# Shared Nodes, Norm Projections, And A Modular Continuation

Date: 2026-09-11.

The shared-node question has a useful exact answer, but several different
matches must be kept distinct. A repeated denominator in the comparison table
was a supplied parameter. Some actual unit directions are shared across the
three fields, with checked construction paths. A scalar norm can also merge
different field elements, losing information needed to determine denominators.
Finally, a congruence branch has a provable 241-adic limit. These statements
do not supply a planar attractor or an improved unit-distance exponent.

The conversation did not identify the precise pair meant by the two
241-squared examples. The examples below establish what can be checked in the
existing construction; they do not retroactively identify that pair.

## Actual Direction Nodes Persist Across The Fields

Use the fields, roots, and q generators from the
[split-prime note](SPLIT_PRIME_BRANCH_GROWTH_NOTE.md). The embeddings send
zeta_4 to zeta_8^2 and zeta_8 to zeta_24^3.

At common denominator 241^2, the exact complete direction sets satisfy

\[
U_4(241^2)\subset U_8(241^2)\subset U_{24}(241^2),
\qquad |U_4|=20,\quad |U_8|=200,\quad |U_{24}|=15,000.
\]

Every smaller direction was embedded and found in the larger set. This is a
full-value comparison of coefficient vectors over the same denominator, not
a comparison of rounded magnitudes. The larger sets retain the earlier
directions and add others. This assertion concerns directions, not equality
or nesting of the particular finite point boxes.

There are also explicit repeated paths to the same direction:

\[
q_{4,1}^k
=\left(\frac{q_{8,1}}{q_{8,3}}\right)^k
=\left(\frac{q_{24,1}q_{24,5}}{q_{24,7}q_{24,11}}\right)^k.
\]

The k=1 identities are the retained relative-norm refinement identities.
Multiplicativity proves the displayed equality for every integer k>=0.
The verifier checks all coefficients and denominator cancellations for k=1..5.
For k=2 the common value is

\[
\frac{29281-50160i}{58081}.
\]

For k>=1 its least denominator is exactly 241^k: the Gaussian prime factors
above 241 have opposite valuations k and -k in q_{4,1}^k. Embedding into a
larger field does not change this least rational denominator, since
O_L intersect K = O_K. Thus future shared directions are specified by an
identity, rather than extrapolated from finite curve crossings.

## A Shared Norm Can Hide Different Factor Paths

Here is an illustrative pair, with both norms taken in the same field K_24.
Write z=zeta_24, a=2+z, and sigma_t(z)=z^t. Compare

\[
A=a^2,\qquad B=a\,\sigma_{17}(a).
\]

Each factor has absolute field norm 241, so N(A)=N(B)=241^2. Their coefficient
vectors in the z power basis are nevertheless different:

~~~text
A = (4, 4, 1, 0, 0,  0,  0, 0)
B = (4, 2, 0, 0, 0, -2, -1, 0)
~~~

The two unit ratios A/conjugate(A) and B/conjugate(B) require different least
rational denominators:

| Construction | Absolute field norm | Least denominator of its unit ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Repeat one factor: a*a | 58,081 | 58,081 |
| Combine distinct factors: a*sigma_17(a) | 58,081 | 241 |

To see why, let P_t be the prime ideal generated by sigma_t(a). The ratios
have valuations encoded by

\[
(A/\overline A)=P_1^2P_{23}^{-2},\qquad
(B/\overline B)=P_1P_{17}P_{23}^{-1}P_7^{-1}.
\]

All these primes are distinct, and 241 is unramified. Repeating a factor
needs exponent two at one denominator prime. The second route needs exponent
one at each of two different denominator primes; the single rational factor
241 already covers both. Exact coefficient multiplication and gcd checks
independently confirm the two denominator values in the report.

The scalar norm records the total multiplicity, while losing its distribution
among the prime factors. Keeping the incoming factor paths recovers that
distinction. This is a concrete reason that agreement under a projection need
not imply agreement in a later construction.

Norms also depend on the ambient field. B has native norm 241 in K_8 and norm
241^2 in K_24, without squaring B. In general, for y in K subset L,
N_{L/Q}(y)=N_{K/Q}(y)^[L:K]. An embedding alone does not square a denominator.

## The Underlying Identity Predicts Other Scalar Hits

Let x be an integer, t=z^3=zeta_8, and define

\[
a_x=x+z,\quad b_x=x^2-t^2-xt^3,\quad
c_x=x^4-1-x^2i,\quad F(x)=x^8-x^4+1.
\]

These are linked by relative norms:

\[
N_{24/8}(a_x)=b_x,\qquad N_{8/4}(b_x)=c_x,\qquad
N_{4/Q}(c_x)=(x^4-1)^2+(x^2)^2=F(x).
\]

The identities follow from z+z^17=-t^3, z^18=-t^2, t^2=i, and t^6=-i.
In particular,

\[
(x^2-i-xt^3)(x^2-i+xt^3)=x^4-1-x^2i.
\]

Multiplicativity gives the corresponding identities for every power k>=0.
Each a_x, b_x, and c_x has norm F(x) in its own field; the k-th powers have
native norms F(x)^k. This does not assert equal underlying elements or
collisions between unrelated choices of x and k.

Some exact examples are:

| x | F(x) | Prime factorization |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2 | 241 | 241 |
| 3 | 6,481 | 6,481 |
| 4 | 65,281 | 97*673 |
| 7 | 5,762,401 | 73*193*409 |

Thus the earlier 73 and 97 branches also occur as factors in this family.
They are factors of the displayed values, not equal to those entire values.

Every prime divisor p of F(x) is 1 modulo 24. Neither 2 nor 3 divides F(x).
For any other divisor, (x^4+1)F(x)=x^12+1 implies x^12=-1 modulo p, so the
order of x divides 24 but not 12. The possibilities are 8 and 24. Order 8
would give x^4=-1 and F(x)=3 modulo p, impossible. Hence the order is 24.

Conversely, every prime p=1 modulo 24 has an element of order 24 in its
multiplicative residue group; it is a root of F. This uses the cyclicity of
that group, as in Theorem 1.1 and Example 1.2 of
[Conrad's cyclotomic notes](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/galoistheory/cyclotomic.pdf).
There are exactly eight such root classes. This characterizes the entire
complete-splitting prime class for K_24 through divisibility. It gives neither
prime values for every x nor a ranking of geometric performance.

## A Genuine Limit After Declaring A Different Notion Of Nearness

The initial hit F(2)=241 has a unique continuation through higher powers of
241 as a congruence. This is another projection; it is not the direction-power
sequence above. Starting with r_1=2, require

\[
F(r_k)\equiv0\pmod{241^k},\qquad
r_{k+1}\equiv r_k\pmod{241^k}.
\]

Write r_{k+1}=r_k+t_k*241^k. Expanding F modulo 241^(k+1) gives the unique
next base-241 digit

\[
t_k\equiv-\frac{F(r_k)}{241^k}\big(F'(r_k)\big)^{-1}\pmod{241}.
\]

Here F'(r_k)=F'(2)=28 modulo 241, so the inverse always exists. The first
digit is 43 and r_2=2+43*241=10,365. The checked continuation is:

| k | Modulus 241^k | Root representative r_k |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 241 | 2 |
| 2 | 58,081 | 10,365 |
| 3 | 13,997,521 | 11,916,970 |
| 4 | 3,373,402,561 | 1,943,574,868 |
| 5 | 812,990,017,201 | 531,567,776,945 |
| 6 | 195,930,594,145,441 | 90,773,459,686,256 |

These are divisibility hits: 241^k divides F(r_k). They are not equalities
F(r_k)=241^k, and do not assert that the exact exponent of 241 is k.

In the 241-adic metric, agreement modulo successively higher powers means
getting closer. The compatible residues converge to the unique root rho of
F in Z_241 with rho=2 modulo 241. It is a primitive 24th root of unity.
Each of the other seven initial root classes gives its own such branch.
This is an application of [Hensel's lemma, Theorem 2.1](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/gradnumthy/hensel.pdf).
For the seed x+z, cancellation uses the embedding of z congruent to -2;
rho above is the x-coordinate root.

The least nonnegative integer representatives r_k grow without bound in the
ordinary real metric: they are nondecreasing and cannot eventually stabilize,
since F has no integer root. Thus this is not an ordinary planar limit or an
attractor for the unit-distance exponent. The modular branch is useful for
predicting factor divisibility; a connection to extra geometric edges still
needs a separate argument.
