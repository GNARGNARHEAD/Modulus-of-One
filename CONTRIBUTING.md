# Contributing To Modulus of One

> Status: contribution guide.

Questions, counterexamples, clearer arguments, and reproducible calculations
are welcome. Start with [the main account](README.md) and
[Core Claims](docs/CLAIMS.md). They distinguish the philosophy, the current
positive-spine implementation, and the questions still open.

For a mathematical contribution, state the question, assumptions, reasoning,
and what the result does or does not establish. An exploratory idea can remain
an idea. If it challenges an existing definition or rule, make that change
explicit so it can be discussed on its merits.

For a computational claim, preserve enough evidence to check it: source
version, command, corpus identity and bounds, construction witnesses, and
relevant controls. Distinguish a value appearing from its confirmation as an
operand. The [research guide](research/README.md) links the individual programs
and their evidence requirements.

Report a software problem with a minimal example, the observed result, and the
expected behavior under the stated rules. The [development guide](docs/software/DEVELOPMENT.md)
gives the standard-library test commands. Run the checks relevant to a change;
do not replace frozen research reports or databases with a new run. Give new
experiments their own paths and identities, including negative results.

This project has been developed with AI assistance, including drafting,
implementation, and criticism. Arguments and executable evidence are the
basis for its claims; model assertions and archived conversations are not
independent validation.

Contributions use the [license for the material being contributed](LICENSE.md):
MIT for software and code examples, and CC BY 4.0 for writing, figures, and
research reports. Identify third-party material and its terms when including
it; do not remove existing attribution or license notices.
