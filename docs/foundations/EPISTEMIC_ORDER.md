# Epistemic Order And The Operator Fan

> Status: canonical foundations note.
>
> This note defines certainty, the continuing operator fan, and the orders of
> support in Modulus of One. The [claim ledger](../CLAIMS.md) states what the present work
> can claim; the [corpus contract](../software/GRAPH_CORPUS.md) specifies the
> narrower positive-spine implementation.

## Immediate Certainty And Admitted Construction

```text
I think, therefore 1.
1 is the only immediate certainty.
```

Here `1` names that certainty. Naming it does not derive arithmetic
cardinality, a featureless whole, or the absence of anything else. Everything
else in the model depends on admitted operations, recurrence, memory,
relations, and later projection from `1`. The immediate certainty alone does
not establish those resources.

Under the adopted philosophical boundary, memory, its content, its
classification as memory, and even a present description of an apparent
memory remain uncertain. Restricting a memory claim to the present does not
secure it. These possibilities can still be investigated conditionally.

The original area inquiry led to an attempt to prove `2` from `1`. The proposed
recurrence route failed as certainty: a previous instance of `1` would have to be preserved and
used again. That previous instance is already once removed from the immediate
certainty. This identifies the gap in that proposal, not an impossibility
theorem covering every conceivable derivation. The phrase "infinite distance
from `1`" describes this philosophical certainty gap, not an implemented metric.

## The Continuing Operator Fan

Modulus of One is not born as positive counting. Its first structural event under the
admitted arithmetic and reuse rules is the operator fan of `1` against itself:

```text
1 + 1 -> 2    expansion
1 - 1 -> 0    cancellation
1 * 1 -> 1    preserved identity through self-relation
1 / 1 -> 1    preserved identity through self-ratio
```

This is the first asymmetry. The operators do not agree on what `1` does to
itself. Their availability and the reuse of `1` have not been derived from
the immediate certainty alone. `2` is the expansion output of the admitted
fan, not a second certainty.

With **each iteration of `1`, all four operations apply across every
available ordered pair, including self-pairs and both operand orders**. The
first fan is not followed by counting alone. In the current positive-spine
implementation, the operands at `U_n` are the confirmed multiples `1..n`;
the graph retains earlier pairs and adds newly available pairs. Retention
and completeness are specified by the [corpus contract](../software/GRAPH_CORPUS.md).

An assumed memory does not select addition. The same admitted unit inputs
allow comparison of accumulation, cancellation, unit multiplication, and
self-ratio. The two-zero divergence names the first pair of readings; it is
not a necessary or exhaustive consequence of uncertainty about memory.

"Absence" and "crossing" are interpretations of cancellation. Arithmetic
zero does not by itself derive an unmarked condition, absolute absence, or
a second epistemic state. Multiplication and division preserve value `1`
through different construction roles. Returning to that value does not
authenticate either witness or make its assumptions immediately certain.
Likewise, `1/1` supplies a self-ratio within the model, not a derivation of
internal parts of one. The open inquiry is developed in
[Otherness, memory, and the negative footprint](OTHERNESS.md).

## Counting And The Signed Field

The positive counting spine `1, 2, 3, ...` is one expansion branch or counted
recurrence projection of the operator-generated field. It is not the whole
Modulus of One core. Zero is the first cancellation point; negative values belong to
continued removal or opposition rather than being optional artifacts.

The conceptual signed-field reading includes:

```text
0 - 1 -> -1
-1 - 1 -> -2
```

These are not legal operand uses in the current positive-spine generator.
Its operands are confirmed positive integers; zero, negative and noninteger
outputs can be retained and inspected, but cannot be reused as operands.
The exact signed-field confirmation and operand rules remain to be
implemented. Existing positive-spine corpora therefore do not represent the
complete signed operator-fan field or prove that zero and negatives are
secondary to positives.

Span, triad and square studies are read through this initial fan:

- `1+1 -> 2` supplies the expansion or first successor span;
- `1,2,3` supports additive-triad inspection within the positive spine;
- `2*2 -> 4` supplies the first nontrivial square emergence.

They are downstream of the first operator asymmetry. This order of explanation
does not make the triad a prerequisite for the `2*2` witness.

Use "operator fan," "positive spine," "signed relational field," and
"removal branch." Do not describe Modulus of One as starting only with upward counting,
strict Modulus of One as positive-only, or positive integers as the whole field.

## Positive-Spine Stages And Operand Eligibility

The current graph-first strict corpus is positive-spine stage-indexed:

```text
U1 confirmed operands: 1
U2 confirmed operands: 1, 2
U3 confirmed operands: 1, 2, 3
...
Un confirmed operands: 1, 2, 3, ..., n
```

The whole field `U_n` also contains operations on those inputs and their
retained relational outputs; it is larger than this operand list. Before
the positive-spine rule has iterated twice, `2` can appear through the first
fan but is not yet confirmed.

```text
construction can precede positive-spine confirmation;
confirmation comes from the positive-spine rule reaching the value.
```

Speculative constructions are still nodes in the admitted runtime model.
Their uncertainty concerns their support and status under the field rule,
not their existence as graph records. They can be inspected, interpreted,
or selected by a probe. They cannot be operands unless the selected field
rule later confirms them. Promotion permits further construction from the
promoted node; it does not confer the immediate certainty of `1`.

```text
Modulus of One speculates from confirmed iterations.
Modulus of One does not speculate on speculations.
```

This operand restriction describes the current positive-spine implementation,
not a completed rule for the full signed field.

## Four Orders Of Support

The orders follow dependencies on the immediate `1`. Descartes' demon
expresses why remembering and iterating `1` cannot inherit its immediate
certainty. Relations such as fractions depend on those multiples; proposed
convergences depend on the resulting relations in turn. Exact finite
arithmetic does not remove these dependencies.

| Order | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Order 1 | The immediate certainty, `1`. |
| Order 2 | Recurrence of `1` through memory and iteration; in the current positive-spine corpus, confirmed positive-spine iterations. |
| Order 3 | Relational emergence from confirmed recurrence, including fractions and multiplicative constructions. |
| Order 4 | Speculation on convergences across the lower orders: projected forms inferred from stable relational emergence, including investigation of infinitesimals and transcendentals. |

Order 2 has raw and counted projections:

```text
1, 1, 1, ...    raw recurrence
1, 2, 3, ...    counted recurrence / counting spine
```

Fractions and unconfirmed future positive whole-number constructions remain
third order under the current corpus rule. Zero and negative values are
relational cancellation/removal nodes; their full signed-field confirmation
status is not yet implemented.

## Confirmation Adds Support Without Rewriting A Witness

At `U3`, both `2` and `3` are confirmed operands, so `3*2 -> 6` can be a
construction. Their earlier presence as value nodes would not suffice.
Before `U6`, the node for `6` has third-order relational support and is not
an operand. At `U6`, the same node gains second-order support through counted
recurrence and becomes an available operand.

The earlier `3*2` witness remains a third-order relation. Confirmation adds
support under the counting rule; it neither rewrites that witness nor
confers immediate certainty. The transition is:

```text
unconfirmed positive-spine whole-number construction
-> confirmed positive-spine iteration of 1
```

A fraction such as `3/2` is also a construction from iterations of `1`, but
it is not a positive-spine counted iteration. It remains third order and
does not become an operand in this implementation. This does not make it
invalid. Nor is an unconfirmed future whole number discarded or treated as
non-Modulus of One while waiting for confirmation.

## Construction And Report Language

Modulus of One should not be explained as starting with arbitrary independent numbers
`a` and `b`. Expressions such as `a/b`, `a*b`, `a+b`, and `a-b` mean a
construction from iterations of `1` related to another construction from
iterations of `1`.

A value appearing is not enough: the same value can appear by different
witnesses. Shared value nodes preserve distinct local operation edges; they
do not select a particular earlier supplying witness for each subsequent
operand use. See the [runtime identity contract](../software/RUNTIME.md).

Implementation terms such as `parent`, `child`, `hub`, `grounded`, and
`speculative` do not override the order model. Read the existing labels as:

```text
grounded Ref(1)       -> Order 1 certainty at the primitive anchor
grounded Ref(N > 1)   -> Order 2 confirmed positive-spine iteration
other values         -> Order 3 unconfirmed or relational construction
                        under the current positive-spine rule
```

A node-level label on `1` does not certify every witness returning to `1`.
Code can keep stable field names; reports must explain their meaning. The
[research protocol](../protocols/research-programs.md#machine-terms-and-modulus-of-one-reading)
maps witness, retention, aperture, controls and holdout terminology.

## Order 4 Is A Proposal, Not Certainty

Infinitesimals and transcendentals share fourth-order epistemic standing:
hypothetical convergence points or forms proposed through inspection of
the earlier orders. This does not identify them mathematically or treat
them as attained endpoints. Circle-like invariants and asymptotic anchors
are also investigated as projected forms.

Here "grounded in inspection" means a proposal has stated support in the
observed relationships. It does not mean confirmed under the runtime's
operand rule. A projected form is an analysis-layer shadow inferred from
a named family of exact graph evidence by an explicit projection rule.

It is non-operational: not a runtime node, operand, confirmed fact, theorem,
or completed object inside strict Modulus of One. Order 4 is more abstract than Order 3,
not more certain. It may guide inspection but cannot participate in
positive-spine strict corpus computation or repair missing witnesses.

A conditional convergence theorem remains a theorem under its stated
assumptions. Those assumptions do not inherit the immediate certainty of
`1`, and the theorem does not insert a completed limit into the finite graph.
The [pi illustration](../examples/PI_FOURTH_ORDER_EXAMPLE.md) makes this
distinction concrete.

Use "Order-4 projected object," "asymptotic inspection anchor," "projected
invariant," or "non-rational projected constant." Do not use "Order-4
certainty," "Modulus of One constructs pi," or "Modulus of One defines the Euclidean circle."
The [Order-4 projection protocol](../protocols/order4-projection.md) owns
the required projection certificate, controls and testing rules.

## Stage Versus Closure Round

Positive-spine stage `U_n` states which whole-number iterations are confirmed
by the selected field rule. A closure round counts passes of arithmetic
construction inside a chosen stage or saved corpus; it is a different clock.
Historical exploratory closure may reuse speculative outputs and must be
identified separately. Neither a pass nor the construction `2*3 -> 6`
immediately confirms `6` under the positive-spine rule.
